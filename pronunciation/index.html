Cheatsheet

How to say it

Italian spelling is close to phonetic: learn about a dozen rules and you can read any street sign in Turin aloud. Every respelling here, and in each sight’s “How to say it” box, uses the same convention.

pah-LAHT-tsohhyphens split syllables
LAHTcapitals mark the stressed syllable
ah · eh · ee · oh · oothe five vowels, never gliding

The rules

Vowels

  • Five pure vowels, always spoken, never gliding: a = ah, e = eh, i = ee, o = oh, u = oo.
  • Say "oh" without the English "-w" at the end and "eh" without sliding into "ay".
  • A final e is never silent.
  • e and o each come open (caffè, like "bet") or closed (perché, nearer French é); Turin speakers blur the two themselves, so a clean eh / oh is safe.
Mole
MOH-leh two syllables, not "mole"
Superga
soo-PEHR-gah
Barolo
bah-ROH-loh

Vowels side by side

  • Two written vowels are usually two sounds.
  • Unstressed i or u next to another vowel shrinks to a y or w glide in the same syllable.
Museo
moo-ZEH-oh
Piemonte
pyeh-MON-teh

DWOH-moh

C, CH, CI

  • c before a, o, u or a consonant = k.
  • c before e, i = ch (CHEH, CHEE).
  • ch before e, i = k (KEH, KEE): the h keeps the c hard.
  • ci before a, o, u = plain ch; the i only softens the c and is not heard.
Castello
kahs-TEL-loh
Città
cheet-TAH
Chiesa
KYEH-zah

KAHCH-chah Palazzina di Caccia di Stupinigi

G, GH, GI

  • The same pattern as c.
  • g before a, o, u = hard g.
  • g before e, i = j.
  • gh before e, i = hard g.
  • gi before a, o, u = plain j, the i silent.

SC, SCH

  • sc before e, i = sh; sci before a, o, u = sh too, the i silent.
  • sc before a, o, u and sch before e, i = sk.

GLI

  • gl before i = ly, as in "million"; the g is not heard.
  • Before other vowels (gla, glo, glu) the g and l are both spoken.

H

  • Always silent.
  • Its only job is spelling: it keeps c and g hard before e and i.

R

  • A single r is one quick tap of the tongue, like the "tt" in a fast American "butter".
  • A double rr is a real trill.
  • Never the English r with the tongue pulled back.
Torino
toh-REE-noh
Barolo
bah-ROH-loh
Ferrero
fehr-REH-roh rr trilled

S

  • s is an unvoiced "s" at the start of a word and next to unvoiced consonants.
  • Between two vowels northern Italians voice it to a z buzz (written "z" in the respellings).

stoo-pee-NEE-jee

Z

  • z is never the English z.
  • It is ts (most common: -zio, -zione, -zza) or dz (often at the start of a word).
  • zz is the same sound, held longer.

  • qu = kw, always.

Double consonants

  • A doubled letter is held for an extra beat, with the first half closing one syllable and the second opening the next.
  • It changes meaning (pala "shovel", palla "ball"), so do not skip it.
  • The respellings write it on both sides of the hyphen.
Lingotto
leen-GOT-toh
Caffè
kahf-FEH
Castello
kahs-TEL-loh

Stress

  • The default is the second-to-last syllable.
  • A written accent (only ever on a final vowel) moves stress to the end.
  • A sizeable minority stress the third-from-last syllable and give no written clue; those are learned by ear.
  • Egizio counts as one of them by spelling (e-GI-zi-o): the final -io merges into one syllable, so you hear eh-JEE-tsyoh.

Città

Genova
JEH-noh-vah third from last
Napoli
NAH-poh-lee third from last
